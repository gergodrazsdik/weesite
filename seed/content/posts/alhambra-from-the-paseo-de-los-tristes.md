After days of looking down from the Alhambra and [across at it from the hill opposite](/blog/the-alhambra-hill-above-granada), I spent my last afternoon in Granada below it, on the Paseo de los Tristes, the walk along the little river at the foot of the hill.

![Square towers and walls of the Alhambra on top of a steep wooded hill, seen from below under a blue sky with clouds](/media/d7eb865de6ad-palace-from-below.jpg "Looking up from the river path")

## A different building

From below, the Alhambra looks less like a palace and more like what it also is: a fortress. The towers stand on the edge of a steep slope, square and plain, and the hillside under them is so thick with trees that the walls seem to grow out of a forest. The decoration everyone comes to see is inside. From here you see only the outside, built to keep people out.

## A slow afternoon

There are cafés along the paseo and a steady stream of people walking up towards the old quarter. I sat for a long time with a coffee, looking up, and watched the clouds move across the towers.

Things I'd tell myself before the trip:

- See a big monument from at least three places: from inside, from across and from below.
- Leave the last afternoon unplanned.
- Don't photograph only the famous view. The awkward angle from underneath was the one I liked best.

I went back to the hotel through the narrow streets, and the next morning I left. This is the photo I printed and put on the wall.
