I spent three nights on Aegina, and every evening I ended up in the same place: a bench on the harbour, facing the water. On the last evening I brought the camera.

![A harbour at sunset with small boats moored in front of houses and a church with a red dome, a mountain behind, all in warm golden light](/media/e7e40f8fdfb6-harbour-sunset.jpg "The harbour and the church in the last of the sun")

## Twenty minutes of gold

Before sunset the sun comes in low and from the side, and everything turns warm: the houses go yellow and pink, and the red dome of the church almost glows. The water is calm at that hour, and the reflections of the small fishing boats hardly move. Behind it all, the mountain is already a blue-grey shadow.

The light doesn't last. Maybe twenty minutes, and then the sun is gone and the colours cool all at once.

## What I did

- I underexposed a little, so the sky wouldn't blow out.
- I walked along the quay until I found a spot where the church rose above the boats.
- I didn't change lenses or rush. I took many frames from the same place instead.

The best picture in the end wasn't taken at the most colourful moment, but a little earlier, while there was still light on the fronts of the houses.

When the light went, I put the camera away and went to one of the harbour tavernas for dinner. I had fish and thought about coming back next year.
