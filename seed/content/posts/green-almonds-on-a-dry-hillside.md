It was an early summer walk, already warm by mid-morning, with hardly any shade on the hillside. Along a stone wall stood a few almond trees, and up close every branch was hung with fruit: green, velvety, a bit like small unripe peaches.

![A branch of an almond tree with green leaves and fuzzy green almond fruits, with dry hills blurred behind](/media/1005e9cec739-almond-blossom.jpg "In early summer the fruit is still green and soft")

## What I hadn't noticed before

I have always paid attention to almond trees for the blossom, because it comes early and you can see it from far away in a bare landscape. I never thought much about what happens next. Now I saw it: the flower becomes a green fruit, the hull dries and splits towards the end of summer, and only then does the nut we know from the shop come out.

At home I read up on it and learned that in some places people eat green almonds whole while they are young, with a little salt. I didn't try one. It wasn't my tree.

## A photo in the wind

The picture took a while. The branch kept moving in the wind, so I used a fast shutter speed and a lot of attempts. In the end I kept one frame where the fruit is sharp and the dry hills behind it turn into a soft blur.

I'd like to go back in autumn, to see the hulls open.
