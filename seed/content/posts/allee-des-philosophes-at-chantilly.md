The park around the château at Chantilly has wide lawns, water and long straight paths. The one I liked most is the Allée des philosophes: two rows of clipped trees along a strip of grass, with a gravel path on each side.

![Two rows of neatly clipped trees along a strip of lawn, with gravel paths on either side](/media/b5fcad111453-tree-avenue.jpg "The avenue on a clear summer afternoon")

## A walk for thinking

I can see where the name comes from. The path is flat and straight, the trees are evenly spaced, and nothing asks for your attention. After a few minutes I stopped looking around and started thinking about other things, which is exactly what I'd hoped a holiday walk would do.

The trees are trimmed into neat rounded shapes, so the light under them comes in regular patches. Walking along, you move from sun to shade to sun again, almost like a rhythm.

## If you go

- Go in the afternoon, when the low sun throws the shadows across the path.
- Walk it both ways. The view back is different from the view forward.
- Take nothing to read. That's not what it's for.

I walked it twice and then sat on the grass at the end. It's one of the simplest garden features I know, and one of the most restful.
