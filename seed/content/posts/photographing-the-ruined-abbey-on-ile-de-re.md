A few days after cycling out to the abbey ruins on Île de Ré, I went back alone, early, with the camera and nothing else to do.

![The roofless ruins of a Gothic stone abbey church, two separate sections of wall with tall pointed window openings, in a field of dry grass under a blue sky](/media/bc1c0a3ec363-ruined-abbey.jpg "Two sections of the church, with the sky where the roof used to be")

## Walls without a roof

What is left of the church stands in two pieces, tall gables of pale stone with pointed window openings, and nothing between them but sky. From some angles they look like a stage set. From others you can still read the shape of the building: the end walls, the line of the nave, where the windows were.

## Working the scene

I walked all the way around before taking a single photo. That's a habit I try to keep, because my first idea is usually the obvious one. Here the obvious photo was straight on, with one wall filling the frame. Going round, I found a spot further back where both sections stood side by side, with the dry grass in front and a band of blue sky above.

A few things I kept in mind:

- Keep the horizon level; old walls lean enough on their own.
- Leave space above the walls, so the missing roof is part of the picture.
- Wait for a cloud to drift behind the stone. Plain blue is a bit flat.

The wait for the cloud took twenty minutes. Nobody else came by. It was the most peaceful twenty minutes of the trip.
