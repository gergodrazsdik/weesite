I spent a morning at the zoo in São Paulo, and of everything I saw, the bird I keep coming back to is a grey crowned crane standing at the edge of a pond.

![A grey crowned crane with a golden crest standing at the edge of a green pond](/media/6cffec95c42d-crowned-crane.jpg "The crane at the edge of the pond")

## The bird

Grey crowned cranes come from Africa, and they look as if someone designed them on purpose: a pale grey body, dark wings, a white cheek patch with a touch of red, and on top a stiff crest of golden bristles. This one walked slowly along the water, stopped, and stood for long stretches looking down at the pond.

It was in no hurry, and neither was I. The plants around the pond made a green frame, and the water reflected the leaves above.

## Photographing at a zoo

Zoos are harder than they look. There are fences, glass, other visitors and a lot of clutter in the background.

- Put the lens close to the fence so the wire disappears.
- Wait for the animal to move somewhere with a clean background.
- Visit early on a weekday, when it's quieter.

I don't love every zoo, but a good one gives you a chance to look closely at animals you would never see otherwise, and to learn their names. I came home and read about cranes for an hour.
