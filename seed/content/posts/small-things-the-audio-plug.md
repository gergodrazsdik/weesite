Some interfaces outlive every device built around them. The 3.5 mm plug is one: three or four metal rings, no protocol, no handshake, and it still works.

![A 3.5 mm audio plug on its cable, on a white background](/media/854ec192d6e7-audio-plug.jpg)

## Why it lasted

- **No negotiation.** An analogue signal needs no driver, so there is nothing to version.
- **Cheap to make.** A plug and a socket cost cents.
- **Forgiving.** Plug it in halfway and you still hear something.

## What software can learn from it

Stable formats beat clever ones. When this site picks a format, such as Markdown for posts or [Atom for feeds](/blog/hello-world), it picks one that will still open in twenty years.
