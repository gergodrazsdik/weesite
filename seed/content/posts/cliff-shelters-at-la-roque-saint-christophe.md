In July I spent a week in the Dordogne, and the hottest day of it I spent at La Roque Saint-Christophe, a long limestone cliff above the Vézère valley with deep ledges along its face. People have sheltered under those ledges for a very long time.

![A pale timber-framed wall with a small window built into the space under a deep overhanging rock ledge, with green trees beside it](/media/e162293717df-cliff-shelter.jpg "A wall built under the overhang, with the rock as its roof")

## Building with what is there

The idea is simple. Where the rock overhangs, you already have a roof and a back wall. You only need to close the front. Along the terraces you can see the traces: holes and notches cut into the stone where beams once sat, and in a few places walls like this one, timber and pale plaster, with a small window looking out.

I liked this wall most because it doesn't pretend. Half of the house is cliff, and the other half is a thin screen made to fit it.

## Cool in the heat

It was well over thirty degrees in the valley that day. Under the rock it was noticeably cooler, and the ledges were in shade for much of the afternoon. You understand at once why people chose this place.

A few notes:

- Wear good shoes; the paths along the ledges are uneven.
- In summer, go in the morning or late afternoon.
- Take time at the edge to look at the valley below. The view is half the reason the cliff was used.

I left with dust on my shoes and a renewed respect for anyone who builds with the landscape instead of against it.
