Mallards are everywhere, which is exactly why most people stop looking at them. On a spring walk along the water in Montreal, one drake let me get close enough to look properly, and I was surprised how much there was to see.

![A male mallard with a glossy green head and a yellow bill standing on a rock beside the water](/media/54e8d306b46c-ducks.jpg "A drake on a rock by the water, in May")

## Looking closer

The green head isn't one green. Depending on the angle it goes from deep bottle green to almost blue, and in some light it has a purple sheen. There's a thin white ring round the neck, a chestnut chest, and grey sides finely marked like a pencil drawing. The orange feet look borrowed from a cartoon.

He stood on the rock for a good few minutes, preening and ignoring me, which made him an easy subject.

## Photographing common birds

- Get down to their eye level. A duck from above is a brown oval.
- Use a plain background. Here it was the water, slightly out of focus.
- Be patient. Birds that are used to people will come close on their own.

I've started treating common birds as practice. Nobody minds if you miss a shot of a mallard, and the next one is never far away. The skills carry over when something rarer shows up.
