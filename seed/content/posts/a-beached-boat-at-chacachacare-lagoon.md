On Margarita Island, off the coast of Venezuela, I spent a slow January morning by the lagoon at Chacachacare. The water was calm, the far shore was a low line of green, and on the sand lay a fishing boat, tipped on its side.

![A small white and red wooden fishing boat lying tilted on a sandy beach beside calm water, with a line of green trees on the far shore](/media/aadfe6db380b-fishing-boat.jpg "The boat, leaning on the sand beside the lagoon")

## A boat at rest

It is a small wooden boat, painted white with a red hull, its mast pointing up at an angle. Green netting lay in a heap next to it. I could not tell whether it had been pulled up for repairs, left for the season, or simply put there for the day. Probably the last.

I liked how it sat: tilted, but not abandoned. The paint was worn but cared for.

## Photographing it

- I kept the horizon low, so the sky and the boat had room.
- A slight angle from the bow showed both the inside of the boat and its shape.
- The light was soft that morning, with thin cloud, which kept the white paint from burning out.

A couple of days later, I found [another boat on the sand](/blog/a-fishing-boat-left-on-the-sand). I am starting to think beached boats are their own genre.
