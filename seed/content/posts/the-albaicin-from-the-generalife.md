Three years after my first visit, I went back to Granada, this time in May. The plan was a few days in the city and a few in the hills around it. I started where I had left off: looking across the valley, but from the other side.

![A dense hillside town of white houses with tiled roofs and dark green cypress trees, with brown mountains in the haze behind](/media/c99ed24e6aaa-hill-quarter.jpg "The Albaicín from the Generalife, on a hazy May afternoon")

## From the gardens

The Generalife is the garden estate on the hill next to the Alhambra. Its terraces look straight across at the Albaicín, the old quarter where I had once climbed to [the viewpoint at San Nicolás](/blog/albaicin-from-san-nicolas).

From here, the quarter looks like a single thing: a slope packed with white walls and red-brown roofs, with cypresses standing up between them like dark brushstrokes. The streets disappear entirely. You cannot imagine getting lost in it, which is very funny if you have tried.

## Spring compared with August

- The gardens were in flower: roses, climbing plants, and the smell of wet earth from the irrigation.
- The air was softer and a little hazy, which flattens the far mountains.
- It was pleasant to walk at midday, which in August had been out of the question.

Two days later I left the city for the hills, to see where all the almonds come from.
