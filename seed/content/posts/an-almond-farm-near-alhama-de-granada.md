After a day on the steep slopes of the Sierra Nevada, I drove west towards Alhama de Granada. The land there is gentler, and the almond trees grow in fields rather than clinging to hillsides.

![Rows of small green almond trees planted in dry, pale brown earth, leading up to a white farm building with tall cypress trees behind](/media/e6071693c6a6-almond-farm.jpg "Young almond trees in rows, below the farm")

## Rows

What struck me first was the order. The trees are planted in straight lines that run up the gentle slope, each one a round green bush on pale, bare earth. At the top stands a long, low white farm building, and behind it a line of tall, dark cypresses.

It looks like a drawing of a farm. The earth between the rows had been worked recently, and there were no weeds at all.

## Thinking about almonds

I eat a lot of almonds. I had never thought much about where they come from beyond "a warm country". Seeing the trees on a mountainside one day and in neat fields like this the next changed that a little.

- The trees here are young and low; I imagine they are easier to harvest.
- The soil is dry and stony, and still the trees looked healthy.
- The cypresses act as a windbreak, or at least look like they do.

I stood at the edge of the field for a while, then drove on. There is not much more to say about it, and that is fine.
