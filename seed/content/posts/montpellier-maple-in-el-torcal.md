El Torcal in Andalusia is mostly rock: grey limestone weathered into stacks and ledges that look like piles of books. Trees are few up there, so when the path turned and I saw this one, broad and green against the blue, I stopped.

![A broad, rounded tree growing among grey limestone rocks under a deep blue sky](/media/42d4961b3539-maple-rocks.jpg "The maple among the rocks, around midday")

## The tree

It's a Montpellier maple, a small maple with three-lobed leaves that does well in dry, stony places. This one had found enough soil in a crack between the rocks to grow wide rather than tall, and it threw the only real shade I'd seen in an hour.

I sat under it for half an hour, ate my sandwich and watched the light move over the rocks. In October the leaves were still green; I'd like to come back later in the autumn to see whether they turn.

## Walking there

The marked routes are easy to follow, but the rocks all look alike after a while.

- Stay on the marked path; it's easy to get turned around between the stacks.
- Take a layer even on a warm day. It's windy and noticeably cooler up there.
- Go slowly. The ground is uneven, and the interesting shapes are everywhere.

It was a short walk, but it's the one I remember best from that trip.
