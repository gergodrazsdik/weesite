I had an hour to kill in Munich and nothing to do with it, so I sat on a bench facing the opera house and wrote most of a blog post on my phone. It was not the first time, but it was the first time I enjoyed it.

![The neoclassical front of the Bavarian State Opera in Munich, with tall columns and a pediment, and a statue on a plinth in the square in front](/media/a8363ca3a439-opera-house.jpg "The opera house on a grey March afternoon")

## What makes it work

Writing on a phone used to mean fighting the editor. These days my setup is boring on purpose:

- Posts are plain Markdown, so any notes app can hold a draft.
- I write without formatting first, and add headings and links later at a real keyboard.
- I keep sentences short. Long ones are hard to edit with a thumb.
- I don't add images on the phone. I leave a placeholder line and fix it at home.

## The building helped

The opera house is a good thing to look at while you think. Big columns, a triangle on top, a statue in the square, pigeons walking around as if they owned it. Nothing moved fast. The sky was grey, the square was mostly empty, and every few minutes I looked up from the screen and then back down with the next sentence ready.

## Afterwards

At home, the draft needed less work than I feared. The structure was there; the wording needed a pass. What I lost in typing speed I gained in focus: on a phone there is no second window and no tab with something else in it.

I won't write everything this way. But the next time I'm somewhere with a bench and an hour, I won't wait for a laptop.
