I nearly walked past this one. Among the spiky leaves in a dry corner of the garden, a single flower spike had come up, yellow-green and covered in small buds that pointed outwards like the bristles of a brush.

![A tall yellow-green flower spike with many small buds, against a soft, blurred background](/media/3ca24cb3a95a-agave-flower.jpg "The flower spike in the late afternoon")

## A slow plant

Agaves take their time. The leaves grow for years before anything like this appears, so seeing one in flower felt like catching something in the act. I didn't know how long it had been growing, and I didn't want to guess, but it was clearly the plant's big moment.

## Getting the picture

The background was a mess of leaves, stems and a fence, so I opened the lens as wide as it would go and moved until the light parts behind the flower turned into soft circles. Then I waited for the wind to drop.

- Shoot with the sun behind or beside the flower, not in front.
- Get level with the spike instead of looking down at it.
- Take a lot of frames; a light breeze is enough to soften the buds.

Out of about thirty frames, two were sharp where I wanted them. That's a normal ratio for me with plants. I printed the better one small and pinned it above my desk.
