On an August walk in the Sierra Nevada in Andalusia, the brightest colour on the hillside wasn't a flower. It was a tree hung with clusters of seeds in orange, red and pale yellow, glowing against the green leaves.

![Branches of a tree with long divided leaves and hanging clusters of orange and red seeds](/media/e373fd4dd04e-mountain-tree.jpg "Seed clusters on a tree of heaven in August")

## A beautiful problem

I had to look it up when I got home. It's a tree of heaven, Ailanthus altissima, originally from China. It grows fast, spreads easily and is now treated as an invasive species in many parts of Europe, Spain included. The seeds that looked so good in the sunlight are part of why it spreads so well: each one has a papery wing and travels on the wind.

That left me with mixed feelings about the photo. It's one of the most colourful pictures from the trip, of a plant that local people would probably rather see gone.

## What I took away

- Pretty and welcome are not the same thing.
- Learning a plant's name changes how you see it next time.
- A plant identification app is worth having on a walk, as long as you double-check what it says.

I still like the picture. I just caption it differently now.
