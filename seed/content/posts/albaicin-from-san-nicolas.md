My first evening in Granada, I did what everyone does and climbed to the viewpoint at San Nicolás. It was August, and the heat had only just started to ease.

![A hillside covered with white and cream houses with tiled roofs and scattered cypress trees, rising to a dry, brown hill under a blue sky](/media/436e81c51b91-white-hillside.jpg "The Albaicín and the slope up to Sacromonte")

## Getting up there

The Albaicín is the old quarter on the hill opposite the Alhambra. Its streets are narrow, steep and paved with small stones, and they rarely go in a straight line. I gave up on the map after ten minutes and just kept going uphill. That works.

Along the way there are white walls, closed gates with gardens behind them, and every so often a gap where you can see out across the valley.

## The view the other way

Most people at the viewpoint face the Alhambra, and with good reason. But I turned around for a while too. Looking along the hill, the houses spill down the slope in every shade of white and cream, with dark cypresses between them, and above them the dry hill of Sacromonte.

- Go an hour before sunset and expect company.
- Bring water; the climb is short but steep.
- Walk down a different way. You will get lost, but downhill always leads somewhere.

Two days later I crossed over to [the Alcazaba](/blog/the-alcazaba-of-the-alhambra), on the hill I had been looking at.
