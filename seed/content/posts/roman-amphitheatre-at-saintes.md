I had expected a grand stone bowl. The amphitheatre at Saintes is something softer: a long oval of sand set into a green valley, with the old arches and vaults coming out of the grass along one side.

![The ruined stone arches and tiers of a Roman amphitheatre around a sandy oval arena, with trees and grass on the slopes](/media/17adda9e02fa-roman-arena.jpg "The arena floor from the upper slope")

## Walking round the rim

There is a path around the top, and I walked it slowly because of the heat. From up there the shape makes sense. The builders used the slope of the valley, so the seating rested partly on the hillside instead of standing on its own.

What surprised me most was the quiet. A few families, a man with a dog, and crickets in the dry grass. It is hard to imagine a crowd in a place that now feels like a park.

## Taking the photo

The light was flat and white in the middle of the afternoon, which is the worst time for stone. I waited until a small cloud softened the sun and took the picture from the upper slope, with the curve of the arena leading into the ruins. I like that the sand is so clean and pale; it makes the old walls look even darker.

If you go in summer, take a hat. There is shade only under the trees at the top.
