Herculaneum is smaller than its famous neighbour, and that is its charm. You walk down into it from the modern town, and within a few minutes you are standing in narrow streets between houses that still have their upper floors.

![Several tall clay amphoras leaning against a plastered wall in a small room, under a dark wooden beam and shelf](/media/155ed262a41d-amphoras.jpg "A storeroom with its jars still in place")

## A room you could almost use

What stopped me was this storeroom. A row of amphoras leans against the wall, the same tall jars with pointed bases that you see in museum cases, except these are where someone left them. Above them is a wooden shelf, dark and cracked, but still a shelf.

It is the ordinary things that make a place like this feel close. Temples and frescoes are impressive, but a cupboard of jars tells you that people kept oil and wine here, ran out of things, and meant to buy more.

## Visiting

- Go early in spring, before the heat and the groups.
- Wear shoes with grip; the old paving is smooth and uneven.
- Take your time in the side rooms. Many of the best details are in corners, behind barriers, in poor light.

The light was the hard part for photography. I braced the camera against a railing and let the shutter stay open a little longer than I would like.
