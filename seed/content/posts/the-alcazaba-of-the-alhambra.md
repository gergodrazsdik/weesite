Everyone talks about the palaces of the Alhambra, with their carved ceilings and pools. I liked them. But the part I keep thinking about is the Alcazaba, the fortress at the western end of the hill.

![Thick sandstone fortress walls and towers on a wooded hill, with flowering shrubs in front and hazy mountains behind](/media/4e44ed96954a-hilltop-fortress.jpg "The Alcazaba in the morning, from below")

## Walls first

The Alcazaba is plain. It is walls, towers, a few courtyards and the outlines of rooms. There is no decoration to speak of. It was built to keep people out and to watch, and it still looks like that from below: heavy, square, the colour of dry earth.

From the top of the towers you see what it was watching. The whole city lies below, and on the other side the Albaicín, where I had stood [two evenings before](/blog/albaicin-from-san-nicolas).

## Practical things

- Tickets for the whole site are timed, and they go fast in summer. I booked well ahead.
- The towers have narrow stairs. They are worth it.
- August is very hot. I went in the morning and spent the afternoon in the shade like a sensible person.

The evening before, I had watched the whole hill [at dusk](/blog/the-alhambra-at-dusk) from the other side of the valley.
