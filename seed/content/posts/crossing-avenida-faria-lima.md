São Paulo is big in a way that is hard to explain until you are standing in it. On Avenida Faria Lima, the office towers go up on both sides and the traffic comes in long waves between the lights.

![A man walking across a wide zebra crossing on a multi-lane avenue lined with trees and tall glass office towers, with cars waiting](/media/577792298337-pedestrian-crossing.jpg "One person on the crossing, between two waves of traffic")

## Waiting for one person

I stood at the corner for a while trying to photograph the avenue. With cars, it was just traffic. Empty, it looked like an architectural drawing. What I wanted was something in between: the scale of the street, and one person to give it a size.

So I waited at the crossing through several cycles of the lights. Most times a crowd crossed together, or nobody did. Then one man walked across alone, mid-stride on the white stripes, and that was the picture.

## Notes for street photography in a big city

- Pick the place first, then wait for the moment. It is less tiring than chasing.
- Stand where you are not in anyone's way. A corner by a lamp post is ideal.
- Keep people small and anonymous in the frame; it is the street you are photographing.
- Count the light cycles. After a few, you know when to raise the camera.

I spent maybe twenty minutes on this one frame. It felt like a long time on a busy corner, and it was worth it.
