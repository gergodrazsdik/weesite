On my second evening in Granada I went back to the hills opposite the Alhambra, this time with a tripod. The idea was simple: wait for the moment when the sky and the floodlit walls are equally bright.

![The long line of the Alhambra's walls and towers lit in warm orange on a wooded hill, under a pale violet evening sky with mountains behind](/media/979db624b962-palace-at-dusk.jpg "Twenty minutes after sunset")

## Waiting for blue hour

The light after sunset changes quickly. First the sky is too bright and the walls look dull. Then, for a short while, the sky turns pale violet, the mountains go soft, and the lights on the walls glow orange. A few minutes later the sky is dark and the walls are just a bright line.

I took a frame every couple of minutes and kept the one from the middle. That is the whole trick, and it still feels like luck.

## What I would do again

- Arrive early for a spot at the wall; it fills up.
- Use a tripod, or at least something to lean the camera on.
- Shoot wide and crop later. The hill is long and the towers are spread out.
- Turn the screen brightness down, so you judge the exposure with your eyes adjusted to the dark.

Afterwards I walked down through the old quarter with everyone else. The next morning I went to see [the Alcazaba](/blog/the-alcazaba-of-the-alhambra) up close.
