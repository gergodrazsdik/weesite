Everyone told me to go up to the Acropolis early, before the heat and the tour groups. It was the one piece of travel advice I followed to the letter, and I was glad I did.

![The rocky hill of the Acropolis in Athens with its walls and the Parthenon on top, under a clear blue sky](/media/12a1f3757f14-parthenon.jpg "The Acropolis from across the city, later the same day")

## Going up

The path is short but steep, and the stone underfoot is worn smooth and slippery in places. Shoes with grip matter more than a hat, though you want both. By the time I reached the top the sun was already strong, and the pale stone threw the light back at me.

The Parthenon is bigger than photos suggest. I walked around it slowly, stopped often and mostly just looked. I took fewer pictures than I expected; up close it doesn't fit in a frame anyway.

## Looking back at it

The view I liked best came later, from a hill across the city. From there the Acropolis looks like what it is: a rock with walls around it and a temple on top, sitting above ordinary streets and apartment blocks.

- Go early, and bring more water than you think.
- Look at it from below and from afar, not only from the top.
- Leave time to sit in the shade afterwards.

I came back down hot and happy, and slept through most of the afternoon.
