In my last week on Margarita I took to walking the back roads in the early morning, before the heat. They are dusty, quiet and lined with dry scrub, thorn bushes and the odd cactus. On one of them stood this house.

![An abandoned single-storey house with peeling white walls, barred windows and a tiled roof, among dry bushes and bare branches](/media/698d502ac042-tile-roof-house.jpg "Somebody's house, once")

## What is left

The walls had been white once and still mostly were, with the plaster coming away near the ground. The windows had iron bars, and behind them nothing but dark. The roof was clay tile, sagging a little. Branches from a tree leaned over it as if they were checking whether anyone was home.

I did not go in, and I would not recommend it: abandoned buildings have rotten beams and things living in them. I stood on the road and took a few pictures instead.

## Why I photograph these

I am drawn to empty houses wherever I travel. They are ordinary buildings, not monuments, and they say more about how people lived than most museums do. Someone chose the colour of the window frames. Someone put up the bars, so there was once something worth keeping safe.

I don't know the story of this one and I didn't try to find out. I wrote down the date and the road in my notebook, and kept walking towards the coast.
