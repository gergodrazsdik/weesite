Near Sainte-Marie on Île de Ré, the way to the beach is a path through the dunes between two wooden fences, sand in ripples, the sea somewhere at the end. I walked this one every morning of our last week there, and on one of those mornings I was thinking about work.

![A sandy path with wind ripples running between two wooden fences through the dunes towards the beach](/media/45920d364aa7-beach-path.jpg "The way down to the beach near Sainte-Marie")

## The path is the point

The fences are there to protect the dunes, but they also make the way obvious. You can't get lost. You don't need a map or a sign to know where the beach is. The path itself tells you.

Good page structure works the same way. When I check one of my sites for accessibility, most of the fixes are not fancy:

- one main heading, and headings that follow in order;
- links that say where they go, not "click here";
- alt text on every image that describes it;
- a visible focus ring, so a keyboard user can see where they are;
- forms with real labels.

## Where my path had gaps

When I tried my own site with only the keyboard, I found a menu I could open but not close, and a search box whose label existed only as placeholder text. Both were easy to fix once I knew. Neither would have shown up if I had only used a mouse.

Sand is hard to walk on, and this path was not perfect either. But it went where it said it would, and that is the bar I try to meet.
