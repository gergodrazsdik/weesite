I went back to Herculaneum two days after my first visit, because one day had not been enough. This time I looked for wood.

![Two blackened, charred wooden shutters standing in a small stone window opening](/media/336828587b4e-charred-shutters.jpg "Shutters, carbonized but still in their frame")

## Wood that should not be here

Wood rarely lasts from antiquity. It rots, burns or gets reused. At Herculaneum, the way the town was buried left some of it carbonized: turned black and brittle, but with its shape intact. You find beams, shelves, a few doors, and these shutters.

They are two plain panels in a small window, slightly open. You can see the grain and the cracks where the wood split as it charred. Nothing about them is decorative. Someone closed them, or half closed them, on an ordinary day.

## What I noticed

- The black of the wood against the pale stone makes them easy to miss in a photograph and hard to miss in person.
- They are smaller than I expected. Windows facing the street were narrow.
- Behind a protective barrier, you get one angle only, so I took a dozen frames of the same view and kept the straightest.

After [the storeroom of amphoras](/blog/amphoras-in-a-storeroom-at-herculaneum), this was the thing that stayed with me most. It is a window. You know exactly what it was for.
