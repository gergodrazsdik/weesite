Aegina is close enough to Athens for a day trip, but I stayed three nights, and I'm glad I did. The harbour town is where the ferry comes in, and on my first morning I walked out along the harbour wall to see it from the water side.

![A row of pastel-coloured houses, cafés and two church towers along a harbour front, seen across blue water](/media/ad095e9b247e-harbour-boats.jpg "The harbour front from across the water")

## The waterfront

From there the town is one long row: pale yellow, white and pink houses, shops and cafés with awnings, and above them two bell towers and a red dome. Small boats tie up along the quay, and in the morning there is a lot of coming and going: fishing boats, delivery vans, people waiting for the next ferry.

## Pistachios

Aegina is known for its pistachios, and you notice. Almost every shop sells them, roasted, salted, in sweets and in spreads. I bought a small bag on the first day and a much larger one on the last.

## Small notes

- Walk the side streets behind the harbour; they're quieter and shaded.
- Morning is a good time for photos of the front, with the sun behind you.
- Bring a hat; there is little shade along the quay at midday.
