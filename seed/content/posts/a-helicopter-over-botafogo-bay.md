I'd climbed up to a viewpoint above Rio de Janeiro to take the classic picture: Botafogo Bay, the city along the shore and the mountains behind, with the statue on the highest peak. I had the frame lined up when a helicopter flew straight into it.

![A small helicopter flying over Rio de Janeiro, with the city and Botafogo Bay below and mountains behind](/media/d5015db41c24-helicopter-bay.jpg "A helicopter crossing the bay on a summer morning")

## Changing plans

My first thought was to wait for it to leave. My second was that it made the picture. Without it, the scene is beautiful but still, a postcard. With it, there's something happening: a small machine crossing a huge landscape, giving a sense of scale you can't get otherwise.

I took a burst as it crossed and kept the frame where its body sits against the city, not the sky. Against the sky it was just a dark shape; against the buildings you can read it.

## What I took from it

- Don't treat everything that enters the frame as a problem.
- A small moving subject can give a big view its scale.
- Shoot a burst. The best position lasts a fraction of a second.

I have plenty of empty, perfect views of the bay from that trip. This is the one I show people.
