The last stop of my spring week in Andalusia was Almería, on the coast. The city has its own Alcazaba, a fortress on a ridge above the old town, and I wanted to see how it compared with the one in Granada.

![A long crenellated stone wall running across a dry, scrubby hillside to a fortress with towers on the ridge, with the sea on the left](/media/dcfec1669878-fortress-walls.jpg "The wall across the valley, with the fortress on the ridge")

## A wall across the hills

The fortress itself sits on the ridge, but what caught my eye was the wall. It leaves the fortress and runs down one hill and up the next, a long line of battlements following the ground. On both sides the slopes are dry and covered in low, grey-green scrub.

Compared with [the Alcazaba in Granada](/blog/the-alcazaba-of-the-alhambra), it feels more open and more exposed. There are no woods around it, just stone, scrub and sky, and the sea is right there.

## Notes

- The climb up to the fortress is short, but the paths inside are long. Give it a morning.
- There is very little shade. Even in May, a hat was a good idea.
- From the walls you can see the harbour and the whole bay.

Almería is drier than Granada, and the landscape around it is closer to desert. After the green hills and almond farms of the past few days, it was a strange and good way to end the trip.
