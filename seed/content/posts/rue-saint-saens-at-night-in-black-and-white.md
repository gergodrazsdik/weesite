I was walking back to my room in the 15th arrondissement late on a November evening when I turned onto Rue Saint-Saëns and stopped. The street runs under the steel structure of an elevated railway, and the wet road reflected everything.

![A black and white night view of a Paris street lined with parked cars and apartment buildings, under the steel girders of an elevated railway, with a car's headlights in the distance](/media/b84cf6a94f8c-paris-street.jpg "Under the viaduct, late in the evening")

## Why black and white

In colour, the picture was a mess. The streetlights were orange, the shop signs were green and red, the windows were every shade of yellow. Each one was fine; together they fought.

In black and white, all of that becomes just light and dark. What is left is the shape of the street: the girders overhead, the rows of parked cars, the lit windows climbing the buildings, and one pair of headlights at the far end where the lines meet.

## How I took it

- I rested the camera on a low wall and used a slow shutter speed.
- I waited for a car to come towards me, for one bright point at the vanishing point.
- I converted it later, and pushed the contrast until the road looked wet again.

Paris is full of famous views. This is not one of them. It is a side street at night, and that is why I like it.
