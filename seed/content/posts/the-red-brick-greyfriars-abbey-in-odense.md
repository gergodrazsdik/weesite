I was in Odense for a couple of days in summer, mostly walking without a plan. On one of those walks I found myself in front of a red brick building with a stepped gable, part of the Greyfriars Abbey site. The date on it is 1872, which makes it much younger than the name suggests.

![A red brick building with a stepped gable, rows of arched windows and an arched doorway](/media/94fd58d36d99-red-brick-abbey.jpg "The gable end on a bright summer afternoon")

## Looking at brick

I don't know much about architecture, but I've started to enjoy brickwork. Here there was plenty to look at:

- the stepped gable, climbing in small stairs to a point,
- the arches over every window and the door, each one made of brick laid on end,
- the slightly different shades of red, which keep a big wall from looking flat.

The light was helping. A clear sky and a sun from the side picked out every course of bricks and every recess around the windows.

## Not knowing the history

I didn't find out much about what the building is used for, and I don't want to guess. That's fine with me. Sometimes I'd rather just look at a building first and read about it later, so that what I notice is my own and not what a sign told me to see.

I'll read up before my next visit. For now, this is a picture of a good wall on a good day.
