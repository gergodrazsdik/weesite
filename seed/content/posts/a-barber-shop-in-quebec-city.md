On a grey afternoon in Quebec City I walked past a barber shop that had just closed for the day. The lights were still on, the chairs were empty, and through the window it looked like a stage set waiting for the actors.

![A black and white photo of a barber shop with two chairs, a long wall of mirrors and a checked floor](/media/8e39704dde24-barber-shop.jpg "Two empty chairs after closing time")

## Why black and white

In colour the scene was busy: bottles, labels, a coloured poster, a bright sign in the corner. Once I took the colour out, what was left was the shape of the room. The checked floor, the row of mirrors and the two heavy chairs in the middle are all strong patterns, and they don't need colour to work.

I don't convert many photos, but I've learned to ask one question: is the picture about the colours, or about the shapes and the light? If it's the second, black and white often does it better.

## Shooting through glass

- Put the lens right against the window to cut reflections.
- Wear something dark, so you don't show up in the glass.
- Keep the camera straight. Leaning lines are very visible in a room full of rectangles.

I'd like to go back one day when it's open and ask for a haircut. It would be nice to sit in one of those chairs.
