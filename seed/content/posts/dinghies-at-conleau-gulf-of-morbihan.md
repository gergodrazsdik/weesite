I was walking along the water at Conleau, near Vannes, when I came round a corner and found these: a long row of small dinghies standing on their ends, packed together like books on a shelf.

![Small dinghies in green, orange, white and teal stored upright in a row on racks beside a house](/media/5167ab9bb5b3-moored-boats.jpg "Tenders waiting for summer")

## Why they are stored like this

They are tenders, the little boats people use to get from the shore to a bigger boat out at a mooring. In spring many of the bigger boats are not out yet, so the tenders wait on land. Standing them up saves space and keeps the rain from collecting inside. I didn't ask anyone; that is my guess from looking at them, and it seems sensible.

## Colour and repetition

What made me stop was the pattern. Dark green, dark green, bright green, orange, white, teal. Each boat a little different in shape, all of them leaning the same way.

I took the photo at an angle rather than straight on, so the row would run away from me and the shapes would overlap. A straight-on shot looked like a catalogue. The angle made it look like a crowd.

A few things I've learned from pictures like this:

- Repetition is interesting only when something breaks it, here the orange one.
- Soft light is kind to colour; it keeps the reds from glowing too much.
- Look at the edges of the frame before you press the button.

I walked on to the end of the peninsula afterwards, but this was the photo of the day.
