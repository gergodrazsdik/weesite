At Pompeii, away from the crowds on the main streets, I found two clay jars lying on the ground at the edge of a ruined house. Round, weathered, one with its rim broken. Nobody was looking at them. I spent longer with them than with most of the famous sights.

![Two round, weathered clay jars lying on dry ground among tufts of grass, one with a broken rim](/media/fc424c101adc-clay-jars.jpg "Storage that outlived everything it stored")

## Simple containers last

Those jars were made for storing ordinary things. They were not clever. They were a shape that holds what you put in it, made of a material that doesn't rot. That is the whole reason they are still there.

I think about this every time I'm tempted to move my writing into something smarter. My posts are Markdown files with a small block of metadata at the top: title, date, tags, a summary. They live in a folder. Any editor can open them, any script can read them, and any site engine I build or switch to later will be able to import them.

## What I avoid

- Content that exists only inside a database I can't easily export.
- Formats that need one particular app to open.
- Clever custom syntax in posts; if a feature needs it, the feature is probably wrong.
- Images without a plain file name I'd recognise.

None of this is new advice. It is just easy to forget when a shiny tool promises to make writing easier.

The jars survived what buried the town around them. My text files only need to survive a few laptops and a change of hosting. A plain container should manage that.
