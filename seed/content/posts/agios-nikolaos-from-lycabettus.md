Lycabettus is the hill you see from almost everywhere in Athens. I walked up it on a cool March morning, mostly for the view of the city, and ended up spending more time looking at one church below.

![A cream-coloured church with a green dome and red tiled roofs, surrounded by pine trees and tall apartment buildings](/media/275dbb9627a7-city-from-hill.jpg "A church of Agios Nikolaos, seen from the slope of Lycabettus")

## The view down

From the top, Athens is a white sea of buildings that runs to the mountains on one side and the water on the other. It is impressive, but it is hard to photograph: too much of the same thing.

Partway down, the view gets closer and more interesting. Here a church of Agios Nikolaos sits in a pocket of pine trees, with apartment blocks rising right behind it, balconies and awnings stacked on top of each other. The green dome and the red roof tiles stand out against all that concrete.

## Walking up

- The paths start from the streets around the base and wind up through pines.
- It is steeper than it looks. Go slowly and stop to look back.
- Morning light comes from the east and lights the city side nicely.

I like cities that let you climb out of them for an hour. You come down with a better map in your head.
