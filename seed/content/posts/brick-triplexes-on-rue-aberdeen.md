Some streets make you slow down without any reason you can name at first. On Rue Aberdeen it was this house: three floors of red brick, a balcony on every level, and stairs climbing up the outside.

![A three-storey red brick house with white and wooden balconies on each floor and outside staircases, with two cars parked in front](/media/694bc0c2df85-brick-houses.jpg "Number 178, on a bright June afternoon")

## Houses with their stairs outside

This kind of building, with a separate flat on each floor, is common in Quebec. Each flat gets its own door, and the upper ones are reached by stairs that run up the front of the house instead of inside it. It saves room indoors, and it gives the street a rhythm of railings, steps and porches.

What I liked here was the colour. The brick is deep red, the balconies on one side are painted white and on the other side left in warm wood, and the trees along the pavement were in full leaf.

## Small observations

- People actually use the balconies: chairs, plants, a bicycle tied to a rail.
- The stairs look steep. I would not want to carry a sofa up them.
- Late afternoon light from the side brings out the texture of the brick.

I walked down the street and came back the same way, just to see this one again. It is not a famous building. It is a good one.
