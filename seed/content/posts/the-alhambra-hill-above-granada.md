I arrived in Granada in the early afternoon, dropped my bag and did what everyone had told me to do: climbed the hill opposite the Alhambra, through narrow white streets, to see it from across the valley.

![The walls and towers of the Alhambra on a wooded hill above the white houses and cypress trees of Granada, with mountains behind](/media/8dd5ccd5fe0f-palace-hill.jpg "The Alhambra from across the valley, late in the afternoon")

## The view

From there the whole thing makes sense at once. The palace and fortress run along the top of a long ridge, with walls and square towers in warm reddish stone, and the slope below is thick with trees. The town fills the bottom of the picture with white walls and tiled roofs, and dark cypresses stand up between the houses. Behind it all, on a clear day, there are mountains.

## Taking my time

I stayed for a couple of hours, which felt indulgent on a first day. The stone changed colour slowly as the sun moved, from pale to a warmer gold. Plenty of people had the same idea, so I found a spot on a low wall a little away from the main crowd.

A few notes for anyone planning the same walk:

- The streets are steep and the paving is uneven; wear proper shoes.
- Go up early if you want a place to stand.
- Bring something warm in spring; it cools quickly once the sun drops.

I visited the Alhambra itself a few days later and wrote about [a path in the gardens](/blog/green-arches-in-the-generalife-gardens-granada) separately. But this first view from outside is still the one I think of.
