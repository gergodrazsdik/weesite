Two days after I photographed [a boat by the lagoon](/blog/a-beached-boat-at-chacachacare-lagoon), I found myself on another stretch of sand, looking at another fishing boat lying on its side. Or perhaps the same kind of boat. Out of the water, they all start to look alike.

![A white and red wooden fishing boat resting tilted on a wide sandy beach beside calm water, under a cloudy sky, with a green island on the horizon](/media/faa1ebeebbfa-boats-aground.jpg "A day of fishing, on hold")

## Same subject, different day

The first picture had a bright, thin sky. This one had heavy cloud moving in from the sea, grey and blue in layers. That changed everything: the boat looked heavier, the sand darker, and the whole scene quieter.

I tried to frame it differently on purpose. More sand in front, the boat further to one side, the low green island on the horizon behind it.

## Why go back to the same subject

- You stop photographing the thing and start photographing the light.
- You notice details you missed the first time: the coiled net, the way the hull is propped up.
- It is a good cure for the feeling that a picture has already been taken.

I have no idea who owns the boat or why it was on the beach that day. Fishing has slow days too, and this looked like one of them.
