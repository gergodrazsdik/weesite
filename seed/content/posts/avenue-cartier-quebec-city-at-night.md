I walked up Avenue Cartier in Quebec City just as the sky was turning dark blue. The street was lined with trees, and above the pavements hung big round lights that glowed in pinks, purples and greens.

![A city street at dusk with large, colourful lit lanterns hanging from the trees above the pavements](/media/28bae3300845-avenue-at-night.jpg "Avenue Cartier at blue hour")

## Blue hour, not night

My first attempt was too late. Once the sky went black, the lanterns turned into bright blobs and everything else disappeared. The next evening I came back twenty minutes earlier, and that made all the difference: the sky still had colour, so the lights had something to sit against.

A few things that helped:

- Stand in the middle of the pavement and let the street lead the eye to the far end.
- Expose for the lanterns, not the shadows. The dark parts can stay dark.
- Wait for a gap in the traffic, or use a car's tail lights on purpose.

## The street itself

Even without a camera it was a nice place to be in the evening. People were eating on terraces, a few were out with dogs, and nobody seemed in a hurry. I ended up spending more time sitting with a drink than taking photos, which is probably the right ratio.
